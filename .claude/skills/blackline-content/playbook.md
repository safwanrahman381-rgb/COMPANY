# Playbook: why the rules are the rules

These are condensed principles from four creator videos (Oct 2026), a Meta-ads study, and platform research. Read this only in REVIEW mode, or when Safwan asks why.

## 1. One hero piece feeds everything (Shane Hummus)

- Make one substantial piece a week, such as a story, teardown or demo walk-through. Cut it into many short posts across platforms. Short posts can't be expanded back into a big piece, so always start big.
- **Positioning sentence:** "I help X do Y (by Z)". Every post should serve that one sentence. A narrow audience with a specific problem can win big contracts from small view counts.
- **Outlier method:** find posts from *small* accounts that did unusually well (roughly 5× their usual reach) even though they were made badly. The idea carried them, so remake the idea properly and keep the angle about 90% the same.
- **Packaging congruence:** the hook, the visual and the first lines must tell one story. Three 7/10 parts pointing the same way beat 10/10 parts pointing different ways.
- **Voice-note ("yap") input:** Safwan talks for 5-10 minutes about his week, and AI writes from that. That's the difference between his voice and generic AI text.
- **Gear doesn't matter.** A phone or webcam is enough, and polish adds little.
- **Mine your own winners:** study what worked, not what failed. Rate ideas with decimal scores plus a confidence level so they're properly ranked.

## 2. Build the system once, then reuse it (Sabrina, Blotato)

- Keep the brand voice, audience, platforms, CTAs and handles in one reusable skill, so they never need explaining twice.
- Write one core post with platform-specific tweaks.
- **Draft first, always.** A human approves before anything goes out.
- Keep a post log (date, platform, link) for later analysis.
- Add real top-performing posts as voice samples.
- **Deterministic quality gate:** a script, not the AI, strips em dashes, counts emojis and so on. Code enforces the rules reliably; an LLM instruction sometimes slips.
- When a tool call fails and gets fixed, write the fix back into the skill.
- A scheduler such as Blotato, Vista Social or GoHighLevel's Social Planner can publish. GoHighLevel's is already paid for.

## 3. Strategy lenses (Callaway, Sandcastles)

- **Content formula = topic + format + visual layout.** Aim for 3-4 topic buckets, 2-3 formats and 1-3 layouts. Test combinations, double down on winners and kill losers. That loop is the whole game.
- **Format finder:** map story structure against visual layout across competitors to find combinations nobody is using.
- **Audience bullseye:** 5 rings from the exact buyer outwards. Narrower doesn't automatically mean fewer views; one ring usually wins on both reach and conversions. Use data to find it.
- **CTA analysis:** test CTA wording, length and how the lead magnet is teased against leads per 1,000 views. In Callaway's data, a few specific CTA traits roughly quadrupled leads. Most tweaks make no difference.
- **Hooks:** study the top and bottom performers for shared patterns.
- **Contrarian take vault:** list the beliefs competitors all repeat and post where you genuinely disagree. Never fake a take.
- **Brand association:** pick a few words you want to own.
- **Uniqueness:** differ on topic, substance, avatar, format or layout.
- **Eras:** when an account suddenly grows, look for what changed: topics, formats or writing.

## 4. Batch statics with taste (Chris Cunningham, ClickUp)

1. Give the context first: who you are, who it's for, likes and dislikes, example posts.
2. Ask for directions before asking for content: 5 carousel concepts, check them, then expand to 5 × 6 slides. Each carousel needs a hook, a flow and a lesson.
3. Design **one** carousel as a style test. Fix the style while the mistake is small, then batch the rest.
4. Run the 5-point visual test in SKILL.md.
5. AI has no taste, so the human approves the direction.

## 5. Ad principles that carry over to organic (Sabri Suby, Hormozi, Meta study)

- Content that doesn't look like an ad gets consumed, and consumption comes before conversion.
- Raw native photos and news-style framing beat polished middle-ground design.
- The creative does the targeting. Meta and LinkedIn read the words and images to decide who sees a post, so name the niche in the copy ("for property managers").
- Proof beats promise, and the whole truth sells, including where you look bad.
- When proof is thin, show the mechanism and let people test the demo themselves.

## 6. Platform facts (checked Oct 2026; re-check with `refresh trends`)

**LinkedIn** ([SocialPilot, Sept 2026](https://www.socialpilot.co/?p=694640), industry figures rather than official LinkedIn data):

- Personal profiles get far more distribution than company pages (about 65% vs about 5% of feed reach).
- Document/carousel posts have the highest engagement, at about 6.6%. Use 8-12 slides.
- Dwell time and comments carry the most weight. Saves are strong.
- The first 30-60 minutes decide a post's reach, so reply fast.
- External links cost about 60% of reach. Putting the link in the first comment is also penalised now.
- Use 1-3 hashtags. Post 2-5 times a week.
- Engagement pods are detected and penalised, and engagement bait is demoted.
- Low-effort, fully AI-written posts get much less reach. What's penalised is the lack of original insight, not the use of AI tools.

**Instagram** ([SocialPilot, Sept 2026](https://www.socialpilot.co/?p=563048)):

- Sends (shares by DM), watch time and likes per reach matter most. Saves also count.
- Since April 2026 the originality penalty covers photos and carousels: accounts that mostly repost others' content lose non-follower reach.
- Keyword-rich captions beat hashtag stuffing.
- Reels get the most non-follower reach. Statics and carousels are fine for nurturing followers.
- Optional, as a test: a simple phone Reel recorded from the same script.

**Inbound behaviour** ([ConnectSafely, 2026](https://connectsafely.ai/articles/ai-sales-strategy-linkedin-inbound-2026), vendor article):

- Post 2-3 times a week in the buyers' own language.
- Treat comments, repeat profile views and engagement as buying signals.
- Follow up with a personal human message about what they engaged with.
- Don't auto-DM, auto-comment or auto-connect on LinkedIn, since it risks account restriction.

**How AI agency marketing usually looks** (general observation, not measured): "I built an AI that…" demos, workflow screenshots, comment-a-keyword lead magnets, and before/after process breakdowns. It's crowded with hype and fake income claims. BLACKLINE stands out by being niche-specific (property and motor), showing outcomes (an enquiry answered and booked) rather than tech diagrams, and being honest about being early.
